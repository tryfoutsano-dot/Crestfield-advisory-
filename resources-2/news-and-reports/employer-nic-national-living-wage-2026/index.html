PAYROLL

Employer NIC at 15% and the National Living Wage at £12.71: what this combination is costing UK businesses right now

Two changes hit payroll simultaneously in April 2025 and continue through 2026/27. Together, they represent the largest increase in employment costs in over a decade.

April 2026 | Estimated read time: 6 min

The Chancellor estimated these changes would raise £25 billion per year. That money comes from employer payroll budgets. For a business with five staff on average salaries, the combined NIC and wage increases add thousands to the annual cost before a single additional hire. Knowing the exact figure and the reliefs available to offset it is not optional for any business with employees.

What changed and when

The employer NIC rate rose from 13.8% to 15% from April 2025 and continues at that rate through 2026/27. The Secondary Threshold — the point at which NIC begins — fell from £9,100 to £5,000. These two changes compound each other. An employee earning £30,000 was previously NIC-liable on £20,900 of earnings. They are now NIC-liable on £25,000. That extra £4,100 taxed at 15% adds £615 per employee per year before the rate increase is even factored in. Full details are published on GOV.UK: Rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027.

What it actually costs per employee

The formula is straightforward: employer NIC equals 15% of earnings above £5,000 per employee. These are the real numbers for common salary levels, before Employment Allowance is applied.

£20,000 salaryNIC-liable earnings: £15,000 → Employer NIC: £2,250 → Extra vs pre-2025: +£674
£30,000 salaryNIC-liable earnings: £25,000 → Employer NIC: £3,750 → Extra vs pre-2025: +£924
£40,000 salaryNIC-liable earnings: £35,000 → Employer NIC: £5,250 → Extra vs pre-2025: +£1,174
£50,000 salaryNIC-liable earnings: £45,000 → Employer NIC: £6,750 → Extra vs pre-2025: +£1,424
£60,000 salaryNIC-liable earnings: £55,000 → Employer NIC: £8,250 → Extra vs pre-2025: +£1,674

For a business with 10 employees averaging £35,000, the combined effect of the rate increase and threshold reduction adds approximately £10,000 to the annual payroll bill before Employment Allowance. This cost does not appear in profit or loss unless someone has calculated it explicitly.

The Employment Allowance: £10,500 many employers are not claiming

The Employment Allowance was increased to £10,500 for 2025/26 and continues at that level for 2026/27. The previous £100,000 NIC liability cap that restricted larger employers has been removed entirely. The allowance is claimed through payroll via RTI submissions and offsets employer NIC as it arises during the year. Sole-director companies with no other employees remain ineligible. Where businesses are connected under common control, only one entity in the group may claim. See GOV.UK: Claim Employment Allowance.

Around 15% of eligible SMEs are not claiming the Employment Allowance, typically because payroll software defaulted to no claim at setup and has never been reviewed. At a 15% NIC rate, this is a £10,500 error sitting uncorrected in the payroll.

Four ways to reduce the impact without reducing headcount

PENSION

Salary sacrifice for pensions

Every £1,000 sacrificed reduces the gross salary on which employer NIC is calculated. At 15%, that saves £150 per £1,000 sacrificed. The employee also reduces their own NIC and income tax.

BENEFITS

Salary sacrifice for other benefits

Electric vehicle leasing, cycle-to-work schemes and childcare vouchers all reduce gross salary before NIC is calculated. The saving is higher at 15% than it was at 13.8%.

DIRECTORS

Review director remuneration structure

The optimal director salary for minimising NIC has changed. At £5,000 the calculation needs to be reviewed, particularly where the Employment Allowance is available.

RELIEFS

Freeport and veteran reliefs

Employers in Freeports, Investment Zones and those hiring qualifying veterans can apply a zero rate of secondary NIC. These reliefs must be actively applied in payroll software.

National Living Wage: what £12.71 compounds

The National Living Wage for workers aged 21 and over rose to £12.71 per hour from April 2026, confirmed on GOV.UK: National Minimum Wage rates. For businesses in sectors where a significant proportion of staff are paid at or near the minimum — retail, hospitality, care, cleaning — this increase interacts directly with the NIC changes. A higher wage means a higher NIC-liable salary, which means more NIC at 15%. A full-time employee on the National Living Wage earns approximately £26,517 per year. Employer NIC on that figure is £3,228 compared to £2,373 under the pre-2025 structure — an increase of £855 per minimum-wage employee, before the wage increase itself is factored into staff costs.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the employer NIC rate for 2026/27?15% on employee earnings above the Secondary Threshold of £5,000 per year. The Employment Allowance remains at £10,500. Current rates are published on GOV.UK: Rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027.
Who can claim the Employment Allowance in 2026/27?Most UK employers with a Class 1 NIC liability. Sole-director companies with no other employees remain ineligible. Connected companies may only make one claim between them.
What is the National Living Wage from April 2026?£12.71 per hour for workers aged 21 and over, up from £12.21. The 18 to 20 rate rose to £10.00 per hour. Current rates for all age groups are on GOV.UK: National Minimum Wage rates.
How does salary sacrifice reduce employer NIC?Salary sacrifice reduces the gross salary on which employer NIC is calculated. At 15%, every £1,000 sacrificed saves the employer £150. Pensions, electric vehicle leasing, cycle-to-work and childcare vouchers all qualify.
Has the optimal director salary changed for 2026/27?Yes. The previous optimal was a salary up to the Secondary Threshold of £9,100 to avoid employer NIC. With the threshold now at £5,000, the structure needs to be reviewed against the Employment Allowance position and total NIC liability.
